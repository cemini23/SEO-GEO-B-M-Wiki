## Narrative

Large-scale randomized experiment with **France Travail** (French Public Employment Service), n = **52,465**. Standard digital job-search assistance pushes unemployed workers to *broaden* occupational search, targeting one source of inefficiency (insufficient occupational diversification). The authors' diagnosis-based analysis of beliefs and search behavior instead identifies a large group of **pessimistic** workers whose binding constraints are **low search effort and low aspirations**, not diversification. For these workers the effective intervention is the *opposite* of broadening: encourage them to search more intensively and apply for better-paying jobs *within the occupations they already consider*. This motivational intervention **increases search effort, raises reservation wages, and improves reemployment** along the predicted margins. Occupational recommendations, by contrast, primarily help workers whose problem is allocating attention across occupations, and they work by **activating existing perceptions rather than correcting beliefs**. The broader takeaway: digital platforms can combine subjective expectations, behavioral data, targeted interventions, and randomized experimentation to diagnose needs and iteratively improve intervention design.
