## Narrative

Electric-field noise near conducting surfaces limits motional coherence and entangling-gate performance in trapped-ion processors. The paper holds stochastic source statistics fixed and asks how a **passive conducting boundary** reshapes the complete spatial electric-field noise operator seen by N ions. From a boundary-potential covariance and the Dirichlet Green function it constructs the N-ion electric-field cross-spectral matrix and its blockwise motional Kossakowski generator. In a parallel slab, billiard unfolding yields an exact return-pair functional; matrix inequalities show how a passive cover redistributes collective normal vs tangential noise coordinates. Diagonalizing the equal-frequency covariance identifies collective environmental noise eigenchannels and a geometry-dependent noise rank; a ten-ion example shows how closing the cover changes participation rank and leading-channel share. Across curved covers, fitted electrostatic decay exponent correlates with shortest specular excess length.
