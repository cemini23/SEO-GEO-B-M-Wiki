## Narrative

**The question.** Does the landing page a GBP listing links to change its local-pack rank?

**The test.** A practitioner listing (a second listing for an NYC personal-injury firm) that was not ranking anywhere. The linked URL was swapped from the homepage to the firm's **bus-accident** page. A second listing was later pointed at the **bicycle-accident** page.

**The result.** The article reports the rank increased in both cases. Its stated mechanism: the presence of the niche term in the **landing-page content and in the URL** raised relevance for that listing, and rank followed. No raw positions are given — the evidence shown is before/after screenshots captured via Places Scout a couple of weeks later.

**The recommendation, in two parts:**

- **Most businesses should keep linking the homepage.** It carries the most authority, backlinks, and relevance. The niche-page tactic applies to businesses with **multiple listings** where a specific page avoids cannibalization — and practitioner listings face filtering, so a dedicated page helps.
- **2025 update — mind the Diversity Update.** The article now advises against pointing a listing at the page that is **already ranking organically** for that term, because it can hurt the organic ranking. Choose a relevant page that is *not* already ranking.

**SEO remit:** `[TENTATIVE]` — a two-listing practitioner case, no raw positions, no control. Treat as a **hypothesis worth testing on the operator's own listings**, not as a ranking rule. The mechanism (landing-page relevance influences the local pack) is consistent with the local-search model already in @concepts/local-pack-rankings.md.

**Cross-check before acting:** the Diversity Update caveat is the part most likely to have changed. Re-read the live article before applying it to a live listing.
