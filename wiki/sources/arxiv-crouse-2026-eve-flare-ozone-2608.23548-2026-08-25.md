## Narrative

Atmospheric photochemical models usually assume a ~9,000 K stellar-flare spectrum at UV-optical wavelengths, but multiwavelength observations span **4,000–40,000 K** flare temperatures with unknown occurrence rates. The paper models a **Proterozoic Earth-like world (0.01 bar O₂) under repeated flaring** across four scenarios — two host stars (K2V, M2.5V) × two flare temperatures (9,000 K, 19,000 K). Hotter flares have a larger impact on O₃ photochemistry for both stellar types; M-star atmospheres are more volatile with rapid O₃ production/destruction swings, while K-star atmospheres are more stable and only impacted by the hottest flares (advantageous for biosignature searches). Simulated 0.2–1.0 µm reflected-light spectra show 19,000 K flares can **produce or destroy O₃ depending on host-star type**: ~2× increase of the 0.2 µm feature for the K2V star vs ~50% decrease for the M2.5V star. The EVE SMEX mission concept would constrain flare temperatures for young FGKM stars to feed improved photochemical models for HWO observations.
