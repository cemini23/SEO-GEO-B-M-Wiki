## Narrative

**What a review block is.** A temporary GBP restriction that stops new reviews from appearing, commonly lasting **30 days**, usually paired with messaging about *"suspicious high-rated reviews."*

**The data.** Tracked over roughly **60 days** across a small set of legitimate businesses. One case shows a baseline of **72 reviews/month** (Jan–Aug 2025), then surges of **179** (Sep, +148.6%), **235** (Oct, +226.4%), and **181** (Nov, +151.4%) — roughly a **2.75×** elevation sustained for about three months before Google acted. Of **595** reviews generated, **342 (57.5%)** were removed. Removals were **selective**, not a full wipe.

**The patterns.** The trigger appears to be **how** reviews arrive, not volume alone:

- Reviews left **inside the business**.
- **QR codes** at counters, tables, or checkout.
- **Bursts** — 10 or more reviews in a single day.

Each is not necessarily a problem alone. The article's reading is that the **repeated combination** raises risk, and that enforcement follows **sustained** elevation rather than an isolated spike.

**What to do.** Submit an appeal through Google's support form **anyway** — in every tracked case the block ran the full 30 days, but logging it contributes data and flags legitimate businesses caught wrongly. The article also advises rethinking employee review incentives and in-store review pushes, and warns a block may persist after an appeal.

**SEO remit:** this is the strongest caution in the wiki against **in-store QR review drives**, a tactic that is otherwise attractive to a barbershop. The wiki's hands-on rules already forbid review gating (@concepts/ai-assistance-guardrails.md). This adds a second, distinct risk: **a legitimate drive can still trip an enforcement block** if the pattern (in-store + QR + burst) repeats. See @concepts/reviews-reputation-management.md for the updated acquisition guidance.

**Confidence:** `[TENTATIVE]` on the specific mechanism — the author is explicit that the in-store/QR reading is **their own assumption**, based on five affected businesses, not a Google statement. The **30-day block length** and the **selective removal** behaviour are the most portable observations. `[NEEDS VERIFICATION 2026-10-01]` for current block behaviour.
