## Raw Concept

Follow-on to the 2026-10-08 rearmament letter. The operator wanted next week's piece to draw from the same war theme. The earlier SWMR long thesis failed an audit (UNSOUND), so this article reframes: instead of a bull call, it explains how the company makes its revenue, and ties Ukraine-proven drone autonomy to the Baltics.

Tone: deliberately non-bearish. It reports mechanics, not a recommendation.

## Narrative

The article is 1,500 words. Structure:

1. Last week's letter (the rearmament trade), then the early-October move: the Latvia incursion on 5 and 6 October, Lithuania's troops, the 6 to 7 October strike on Ukraine.
2. Swarmer (NASDAQ: SWMR): drone-swarm software, combat-proven in Ukraine, Erik Prince chairman.
3. How it makes its revenue: three parts. Licenses (per-drone, $750 to $30,000). One financed first deployment through the SkyKnight group, which is about 99 percent of Q2 revenue. An acquisition (Ratel Robotics, not closed).
4. The Baltics: combat-tested autonomy for contested electromagnetic conditions is a NATO eastern-flank need.
5. What to watch.

Key facts, all from the filings: Q2 2026 revenue $216,413; gross margin 84.8 percent but cost of revenue only about $33,000; the $4.9M versus $3.9M round-trip; Ratel audited FY2025 net sales $18.6M; the $86.6M contract liability; going-concern doubt belongs to Ratel.

Audit: a free multi-model audit (grok, deepseek, opencode, nemotron) returned three WARN and two PASS. The six consensus fixes are applied. Paid spend $0.

Voice: a grok voice pass plus a hand pass (10 Oct) killed the "Not X. It is Y." constructions and varied the repeated "The company" / "It is" openers. Em dashes were already zero. A Prince chairman line was added so the body matches the "Erik Prince" tag.

Title options considered: "How a $205 Million Drone Stock Makes Its Revenue" (chosen), "The Round-Trip".
