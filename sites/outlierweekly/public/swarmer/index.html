How a $205 Million Drone Stock Makes Its Revenue

Swarmer (NASDAQ: SWMR) sells drone-swarm software, in Ukrainian combat since 2024 by the company's account, chaired by Erik Prince. The title is the question the letter answers: what does a small defense software company actually book? In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was $216,413, and about 99 percent of it came from one counterparty group.

The letter

The 12 October 2026 issue How a $205 Million Drone Stock Makes Its Revenue is the canonical write-up. It reads the business off the SEC filings and splits the revenue into three parts. The first is licenses: a per-drone right to run the software on hardware Swarmer did not make, priced from $750 to $30,000 a unit, which is why the gross margin looked high, 84.8 percent, though cost of revenue was only about $33,000 because the hardware cost sat in an advance. The second is a single arrangement with a counterparty group called SkyKnight, worth about 99 percent of second-quarter revenue; Swarmer signed to pay the group about $4.9 million against the group's own consideration of about $3.9 million, and the filing says it could not substantiate the fair value of $2.4 million of services, so that amount reduces revenue when it is paid. The third is Ratel Robotics, a Ukrainian maker of combat ground robots that Swarmer agreed to buy, with audited 2025 net sales of $18.6 million, a contract liability of $86.6 million, and going-concern doubt of its own; the deal has not closed. The letter then ties Ukraine-proven autonomy to the Baltics, where Poland spends about 4.7 percent of its economy on defense and Lithuania about 5.3, on NATO's 2026 estimates.
