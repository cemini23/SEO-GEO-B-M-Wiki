Methodology

How an Outlier Weekly issue is built. One subject. The numbers in the open. A conclusion a reader can check.

What an issue is

Each issue takes one subject. The subject can be a metal, a license, a listing, a lab, a ticket price, or a product the operator ships. Most weeks the subject is investing or AI. Some weeks it is a market or a tool that sits next to that desk.

The letter shows the numbers that decide the write-up. It says where the public story and those numbers disagree. It ends with one conclusion: a position, a pass, or a product fact.

How an issue is built

  • Pick one subject and name it in the title.
  • Write the numbers that matter: a price, a license, a displayed odds line, or a dated fact.
  • Say where the public story and those numbers disagree.
  • End with one conclusion.
  • Point at the hub on this site when the subject has one.

If this page and a letter disagree, the letter wins. The letter is dated. This page is the standing description.

Where this started

The letter launched in May 2026 on rare-event contracts. Issue 1 and Issue 2 each priced one Polymarket or Kalshi market with three formulas: a Poisson base rate, a Shannon / KL information gap, and a Taleb fat-tail size. Those issues used a 40 / 35 / 25 weighting. The inputs, the math, and the call stayed in the letter.

That system is the origin of the desk. Later letters keep the same habit — one subject, numbers in the open. A later letter does not have to be a prediction-market contract. The May weights belong to those first issues. A tungsten note, a parlay note, or a product note states its own numbers.

The formula write-up, with the worked example, stays on the public methodology mirror: cemini23.github.io/outlier-weekly-methodology. Read that page as the record of the first system. Issue 1 and Issue 2 are the dated applications.

What this page is not

  • Not a black box. The numbers an issue uses are in the letter.
  • Not a guarantee. A number that disagrees with the crowd can be wrong. The letters record both outcomes.
  • Not investment advice. The letters are research and commentary, not a solicitation to buy, sell, or bet.
